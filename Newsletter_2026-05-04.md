## The World

**The Hormuz disruption, still ongoing.** Six weeks after the US-Iran ceasefire, tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains well below normal. Brent crude peaked at $126 in March; US gas prices jumped 30 cents in a week. Mohamed El-Erian's warning last week: four to eight weeks before this tips into global recession. Growth forecasts are already being revised to 2.8%. And it's not just an oil story — sulfur, graphite, and aluminum (all clean energy inputs) flow through the strait too. [Read →](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-iran-war-hormuz-closure-oil-shock/)

**US economy: slowing under the surface.** Separately from Hormuz: tariff-driven inflation is showing up in consumer prices. Real consumer spending is projected to slow to 2.1% this year, down from 2.7% in 2025. The job market has cooled. GDP growth is forecast around 2.2% — fine on paper, but with meaningful downside if oil stays elevated. [Read →](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/economy-2026-outlook-affordability-rates-jobs-markets/)

**AI becomes labor.** The consistent theme across every AI announcement this week: tools are becoming workers. OpenAI expanded GPT-5.5 to Amazon's cloud, loosening its Microsoft exclusivity. Anthropic released Mythos to select partners. The Pentagon struck deals with seven Big Tech companies. Google committed up to $40B more into Anthropic, including 5 GW of compute over five years. The competition is no longer about benchmarks — it's about what can actually complete a task end to end. [Read →](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40b-in-anthropic-in-cash-and-compute/)

## Clean Energy & Tech

**PJM's queue: gas is back.** PJM's first reformed interconnection cycle drew 811 projects, 220 GW total. Natural gas leads at 106 GW — nearly half of capacity. Solar: just 15 GW. This reverses 2022, when renewables dominated. The shift reflects years of queue delays killing the renewable backlog, data centers demanding firm dispatchable power fast, and gas being ready to move. Storage (67 GW) is still large, but the queue is a snapshot of 2026, not 2022's energy transition thesis. [Read →](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-interconnection-queue-gas-solar-nuclear/818824/)

**Power, not policy, is the conversation.** Across SF, Asia, and Europe last week, every climate tech discussion circled back to the same thing: demand. Data centers need gigawatts, and carbon credits don't solve a procurement problem. The companies gaining traction are the ones solving for firm power, grid resilience, and speed. The ones pitching decarbonization as the lead are losing the room. [Read →](https://spectrum.ieee.org/firms-bet-climate-tech)

**Geothermal's test.** Fervo Energy filed its S-1 — the first enhanced geothermal company to go public. Same week, X-energy (Amazon-backed SMR developer) debuted at $11.5B, roughly four times Fervo's valuation. Nuclear is being priced on AI enthusiasm; geothermal on what it can actually deliver. Fervo's Utah project needs to hit 100 MW by early 2027. That's the number to watch.

**A permitting win, with caveats.** A federal judge struck down five Trump administration tactics blocking renewable permitting, including a requirement that Interior Secretary Burgum personally sign off on all major approvals. Real ruling, but the permitting environment has been chilled for months of deliberate delay — and the Supreme Court's recent posture on injunctions leaves the win uncertain. [Read →](https://advancedenergyunited.org/press-releases/pjm-queue-reopening-april-2026/)

**Desalination gets a second look.** A cluster of startups is promising cheaper, lower-energy water purification — desalination without the intensity of traditional reverse osmosis. Water stress and energy stress are accelerating in the same geographies, and investors are starting to treat them as linked problems. [Read →](https://heatmap.news)

**Japan's geothermal commitment.** Japan announced $690M in subsidies for next-generation geothermal, covering up to two-thirds of drilling and survey costs for enhanced and supercritical projects through 2030. Japan sits on enormous geothermal resources and has been slow to develop them. This is a meaningful shift.

**Indonesia mandates B50.** All Indonesian biodiesel users must use fuel that is at least 50% palm oil by 2028. Framed partly as climate policy; driven mostly by energy security after Hormuz exposed import dependence.

**Deals.** Blue Energy (prefab nuclear, MD) $380M Series A. ILOS (solar developer, Germany) $235M project finance. Evren (renewables, India) $200M. Fervo filed S-1. X-energy debuted at $11.5B.
